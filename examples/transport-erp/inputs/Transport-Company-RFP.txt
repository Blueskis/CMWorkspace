2.3 Change Management
2.3.1 Change Management Strategy and Approach
2.3.1.1 Change Management StrategyThe Tenderers should provide a clear change management strategy aligned with the ERP transformation objectives. The strategy must clearly outline:· The expected scope and scale of organisational change.· Transformation principles that guide behaviour, decision-making, and communication.· Alignment with cross-BU transformation and ERP-Maximo co-existence scenarios.· Definition of user adoption objectives and overall change outcomes.· Employee change management framework to ensure smooth adoption of the System and facilitate change enablementThe Tenderers should provide Change Management Strategy and Framework for their recent ERP project.
2.3.1.2 Change Management ApproachThe Tenderers should propose and deliver a comprehensive Change Management approach to ensure effective adoption of the target ERP Solution, minimize business disruption, and enable users to transition successfully from the legacy system to the new ERP platform.The Change Management approach should address organizational, process, role, and behavioural impacts arising from the ERP migration and should be fully integrated with the overall Project governance and delivery methodology.The Tenderers should describe the approach and the steps they will follow. They should submit Change Management Approach Overview from their previous latest ERP project.
2.3.1.3 Change Management MethodologyThe Tenderers should define the overall change management methodology and framework.· Structured phases of change management (e.g., Assess, Plan, Execute, Reinforce).· Key deliverables across each phase (high-level only, without detailed plans).· Overall approach to managing adoption and organisational transition.
2.3.1.4 Change Management Plan and ActivitiesThe Tenderers should devise a Change Management Plan that sets the overall direction and pace of the entire change management journey as part of the Project implementation with the following change management activities but not limited to:· Alignment of change activities with major ERP milestones (e.g., SIT, UAT, Go-Live).· Definition of key change milestones and readiness checkpoints.· Definition of roles and responsibilities (SI / Client / Joint).· Change governance structure (e.g., Change Board, escalation paths, decision-making model).· Change organisation structure and reporting lines.· How change management is coordinated across functional, technical, integration, and data workstreams.· Approach to ensure consistency of change delivery across business units.· Identification of key personnel to be involved with Key roles and competencies required.· High-level resourcing and allocation across Project phases.· Transition Plan from current system to new System from technical and business aspects. Technical aspect should cover the coexistence strategy with legacy system and plan to address dependencies between legacy and new System while business aspect should cover change management of end users, ORGANISATION X technical team and support team· Approach for knowledge transfer and long-term adoption sustainability.· High-level post Go-Live support model.
2.3.2 Stakeholder Analysis and Engagement
The Tenderers should identify key stakeholders who are critical to the success of the Project and involve them in activities that prepare and energise the organisation for the change. This includes understanding stakeholder expectations, required business outcomes, concerns, and anticipated risks, as well as ensuring feedback loops are closed so that concerns are addressed.
2.3.2.1 Identification of Key Stakeholder GroupsThe Tenderers should conduct a comprehensive identification and classification of all key stakeholder groups that will be impacted by, involved in, or required to support the ERP migration. This includes stakeholders across all affected business units, operational domains, and organisational roles. At a minimum, the Tenderers must consider and assess stakeholder groups such as:· Finance users (e.g., accounts payable/receivable, general ledger, fixed assets, treasury)· Procurement and sourcing teams (e.g., buyers, category managers, contract administrators)· Inventory and warehouse controllers (e.g., storekeepers, material planners, logistics personnel)· Operational managers and supervisors across functional departments· IT and system support staff, including system administrators and technical SMEs· Project governance stakeholders (e.g., PMO, process owners, steering committee members)· Change champions, end users, and ancillary support roles directly or indirectly affected by new processes and system functionalitiesAs part of this submission, the Tenderers should provide the following:
2.3.2.1.1 Stakeholder Register / Stakeholder ListA detailed and comprehensive register that includes, at minimum:· Stakeholder group name, business unit or department· Key roles and job functions within each group· Level of impact from the ERP migration (e.g., high, medium, low)· Primary concerns, interests, or expectations, High-level engagement needs
2.3.2.1.2 Stakeholder MapA visual stakeholder mapping that clearly illustrates:· The organisational units impacted, Key roles and stakeholder clusters· Interdependencies between business functions and processes· Impact pathways showing how each group is affected by the ERP migration· The degree of influence and interest of each stakeholder group
2.3.2.1.3 Descriptive Profiles of Each Stakeholder GroupA concise narrative description for each key stakeholder group, including:· Primary business functions and responsibilities, relevance to the ERP Solution, including processes and modules they interact with· Expected nature of the change impact (e.g., procedural, system usage, reporting changes)· Any dependencies or constraints, Potential risks or challenges related to stakeholder readiness
2.3.2.1.4 Stakeholder Impact and Influence MatrixThe Tenderers should provide a Stakeholder Impact and Influence Matrix, illustrating how stakeholder groups will be categorised and prioritised. At minimum, the matrix should include Stakeholder group name, Stakeholder's details, Readiness and resistance score.
2.3.2.2 Stakeholder Impact Assessment
2.3.2.2.1 Assessment and Engagement ApproachThe Tenderers should conduct a comprehensive Stakeholder Impact Assessment to evaluate how the ERP migration will affect all identified stakeholder groups across the organisation. The assessment must be systematic, evidence-based, and aligned with recognised change-management practices. It should consider not only process and system changes, but also behavioural, organisational, and readiness dimensions to ensure that stakeholders are adequately prepared and supported throughout the ERP transition.
2.3.2.2.2 Stakeholder Impact Assessment DimensionsThe Tenderers should assess each stakeholder group across, but not limited to, the following key dimensions:
2.3.2.2.2.1 Stakeholder Influence and Decision Authority· Degree of influence over business processes, policies, or implementation decisions.· Role in shaping or approving ERP-related decisions.· Potential to champion or obstruct Project outcomes.
2.3.2.2.2.2 Readiness for Change· Current awareness of the ERP initiative, Maturity of existing processes and systems used by the group.· Competency levels relevant to the new ERP capabilities.· Overall preparedness to adopt new workflows, tools, and responsibilities.
2.3.2.2.2.3 Potential Resistance Factors· Anticipated behavioural or cultural resistance, Perceived loss of authority, control, or established practices.· Historical challenges with technology adoption.· Any structural or organisational constraints that may impact acceptance.
2.3.2.2.2.4 Expected Level of Impact· Extent of change to daily tasks, reporting responsibilities, or decision-making processes.· Required adjustments to competencies, roles, or process ownership.· Nature of technology or interface changes introduced by the ERP Solution.· Degree of operational disruption expected during migration and post Go-Live stages.The Tenderers must analyse these factors holistically to determine the level and type of intervention required for each stakeholder group.
2.3.2.3 Stakeholder Engagement
2.3.2.3.1 Stakeholder Engagement ApproachThe Tenderers should propose a customised engagement approach for each stakeholder group based on the results of the impact assessment. The engagement approach should include:
Engagement Objectives: What the engagement intends to achieve for each stakeholder group
Engagement Strategy and Approach: Tailored engagement strategies with frequency and modality of engagement
Role-Specific Engagement Activities: Activities designed for high-impact/high-influence/low-influence groups with special support measures
Communication and Training Alignment: Tying of engagement activities into the overall training, communication, change-enablement plans and feedback loop for refinement
Monitoring & Adjustments: Mechanisms to monitor engagement effectiveness
2.3.2.3.2 Stakeholder Engagement Throughout the Transformation LifecycleThe Tenderers should define and implement comprehensive mechanisms to engage stakeholders throughout the full ERP transformation lifecycle. These mechanisms must ensure that all stakeholder groups remain informed, aligned, involved, and supported across the design, build, testing, Go-Live, and post Go-Live optimisation phases. Engagement approaches must be structured, repeatable, inclusive, and tailored to the unique needs and roles of each group to enable successful adoption of the ERP Solution.
2.3.2.4 Resistance Management
2.3.2.4.1 Resistance Management and User AdoptionThe Tenderers should define and implement structured measures to identify, assess, mitigate, and monitor stakeholder resistance throughout the ERP transformation. The Tenderers must also propose a comprehensive approach to encourage user adoption of the new ERP system, ensuring that employees across all business units understand the value, capabilities, and operational implications of the new Solution. The approach should be proactive, data-driven, and integrated with the broader change-management and engagement strategy.
2.3.2.4.2 Resistance Identification and Assessment MeasuresThe Tenderers should outline the methods and mechanisms that will be used to identify resistance early and accurately. These measures should include, but not be limited to:
Stakeholder Sentiment Assessment: readiness assessments designed to detect concerns or emotional barriers
Behavioural and Operational Resistance Indicators: Identification of deviations in expected contributions or hesitance in decision-making
Structured Feedback Channels: Formal and informal feedback mechanisms, Documentation and categorisation of concerns
Risk Categorisationo Classification of resistance types (e.g., passive, active, process-driven, cultural).o Assignment of risk severity and impact rating for targeted intervention planning.The Tenderers should provide Resistance Management Approach and User Adoption Strategy document as part of the proposal submission.
2.3.2.5 Leadership Alignment and SponsorshipThe Tenderers should ensure alignment with business leaders and Project sponsors to support the ERP transformation and reinforce change and adoption across the organisation.The Tenderers should submit:a) Leadership and sponsor engagement approach detailing the roles of executives and sponsors, expectations for their involvement during key phases of the ERP programme, the mechanisms to engage and brief them, and the cadence for leadership touchpoints, reviews, and decision-making interactions.b) Sample leadership briefing or communication materials demonstrating how key messages, programme updates, change impacts, risks, and adoption expectations will be communicated to the leadership team and sponsors to equip them to effectively advocate for the transformation.c) Governance and escalation structure related to stakeholder issues outlining leadership responsibilities in addressing stakeholder resistance, resolving cross-functional change challenges, approving major change decisions, and supporting the timely escalation and closure of stakeholder-related risks or issues during the ERP transformation.

These mechanisms must support transparent communication, continuous improvement, and timely issue resolution. The Tenderers should ensure that feedback is captured, analysed, prioritised, and addressed in a manner that supports effective stakeholder engagement and promotes a positive adoption experience of the new ERP system.
2.3.2.6.1 Feedback Collection ApproachThe Tenderers should provide a detailed description of the mechanisms that will be used to capture stakeholder feedback during the ERP programme. At minimum, the approach should include the following channels:
Surveys and Pulse Checks
Workshops, Focus Groups, and User Forums
Helpdesk, Service Desk, and Hypercare Inputs
Change Champion and Super User Networks
Digital Feedback ChannelsThe Tenderer's approach must ensure that feedback mechanisms are easily accessible, anonymous where required, and suitable for both end users and management stakeholders.
2.3.2.6.2 Process for Reviewing, Prioritising, and Acting on FeedbackThe Tenderers should describe a structured Feedback Management Process outlining how feedback will be evaluated, escalated, and resolved. At minimum, the process should include:
