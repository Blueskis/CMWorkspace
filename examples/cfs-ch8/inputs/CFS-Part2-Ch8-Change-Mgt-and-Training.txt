1. INTRODUCTION 
1.1. Introduction 
1.1.1. The Contractor shall lead and execute change management activities to ensure 
stakeholder readiness prior to each phase of Licensee onboarding. The Contractor 
shall adapt change management approaches as needed throughout the phased 
implementation up till the end of the Solution Warranty Period (SWP) for the last 
Licensee onboarded. 
1.1.2.  The stakeholders include but are not limited to the following: 
(a) Senior stakeholder groups including executive project sponsors, leadership, 
board members, service professionals, administrators and staff; 
(b) Policy divisions, operational teams, and owners of existing systems affected 
by or replaced by the new Solution; 
(c) Authority users, nominated personnel, and authority -nominated technical 
personnel; 
(d) Functional business units such as Finance and Procurement with processes 
and systems directly impacted by the Solution; 
(e) Technical and project delivery teams, including ICT representatives, data 
owners, system integration leads, cybersecurity and infrastructure personnel; 
(f) End users of the Solution across all organisational levels, including identified 
superusers and change champions who will support training and adoption 
activities; 
(g) External partners and third -party providers involved in service delivery, 
solution integration, or ongoing system support after each Licensee has 
onboarded. 
1.1.3. The Contractor shall develop a stakeholder engagement strategy and Change 
Management plan to address the varying levels of concerns across different 
stakeholder groups. 
1.1.4. The Contractor shall ensure that stakeholders understand the need for change and 
the impact of new working procedures  to promote positive reactions and 
enthusiasm for adopting the Solution. 
1.1.5. The Contractor shall ensure stakeholders are well-informed and aligned on project 
activities to enable a smooth transition with minimal operational disruptions 
throughout all project development stages.

2. CHANGE MANAGEMENT TEAM 
2.1. Team Composition and Structure 
2.1.1. The Tenderer shall propose a Change Management Team led by a Contractor 
Change Manager. Refer to Part 2  Chapter 4 , Clause 6 for detailed role 
descriptions and required skillsets. The Change Management Team shall possess 
appropriate expertise and experience to support and partner with the Authority in 
delivering effective change management throughout the project lifecycle. 
2.1.2. The Tenderer shall clearly define the reporting hierarchy and organisational 
structure of the Change Management Team, including reporting relationships with 
the Authority's project team and other contractor teams. 
2.2. Team Management and Controls 
2.2.1. The appointment of the Change Manager and key Change Management Team 
members shall be subject to the Authority's approval. 
2.2.2. The Contractor shall ensure minimal changes to Change Management Team 
composition throughout the Contract Period. The Contractor shall not change the 
Change Manager or key team members without prior written approval from the 
Authority. 
2.2.3. Where subcontractors are engaged for change management activities, the 
Contractor shall detail how these subcontractors will be managed and trained to 
ensure obligations are met. 
2.3. Roles and Responsibilities 
2.3.1. The Change Manager shall collaborate with the development and design team to 
ensure change management considerations are integrated throughout the 
development process. 
2.3.2. The Change Manager shall provide ongoing guidance to the development team on 
stakeholder requirements and user experience factors that will facilitate Solution 
adoption. 
2.3.3. The Change Management team shall review and ensure consistency and coherence 
in all messaging and communications materials (including emails, presentations, 
training materials, and announcements) prior to seeking Authority approval and 
releasing to stakeholders.

3. CHANGE MANAGEMENT SCOPE 
3.1. Change Management Plan 
3.1.1. The Tenderer shall propose a Change Management Plan that covers the entire 
project lifecycle, including: (a) implementation stage; (b) post go -live BAU 
operations stage (including Performance Guarantee Period (PGP) and SWP) ; and 
(c) maintenance services stage, encompassing new releases and patches that affect 
stakeholders. 
3.1.2. The Tenderer shall provide a comprehensive Change Management proposal in its 
Tender Offer, including the following: 
(a) Change Management Strategy and Approach that includes but not limited to 
the following components: 
i) Planning – Design, scheduling, and timeline for intended outcomes, 
changes, and announcements, organised into phased stages. This shall 
include a capability building plan to equip key stakeholders and the 
change management team with the requisite mindset and skills t o 
support change management activities, including just-in-time coaching 
and mentoring; 
ii) Evaluation – Assessment of risks and benefits at each stage, with 
recommended implementation approaches for each phase. This 
includes but not limited to change impact analysis across the Authority 
and Licensees; 
iii) Communication Strategy – Development of a compelling change 
narrative that establishes context, initiates calls to action, and explains 
the transformation journey towards the desired vision; 
iv) Implementation Schedule – Detailed timeline for executing the change 
management plan according to project milestones; 
v) Post-Implementation Review – Monitoring and evaluation of change 
implementation effectiveness, with recommendations for adjustments 
where necessary. This includes developing pulse check surveys to track 
project success measures and change progress, conducted every six 
months or aligned with project milestones. 
vi) Change Agent Identification and Training – Identification of suitable 
change agents within the organisation and development of a 
comprehensive training plan for the identified change agents. 
(b) Change Management Team Structure, including the roles and responsibilities 
of the Contractor team, Subcontractors (if proposed) , the Authority  and 
Licensees;

(c) Detailed Stakeholder Engagement Plan, including methods of engagement

Management Plan before execution at different development stages.

3.3. Implementation Requirements 
3.3.1. The Contractor shall work with the Authority to leverage policy makers, 
stakeholders, and change networks to develop and customise communications and 
materials. 
3.3.2. The Contractor may use communication channels such as newsletters, videos, and 
road shows. The Change Management team shall track and coordinate all 
communications from all channels and sources. 
3.3.3. The Contractor shall prepare all engagement and communication materials 
(including EDMs, posters, briefing slides ) and obtain the Authority’s approval at 
least five (5) working days before engagement commencement. The Authority 
reserves the right to modify, add to, and use the materials at any time. 
3.3.4. The Contractor shall plan and prepare to execute the Change Sustenance Plan post 
SWP of  the last Licensee onboarded , if the option  under Clause 4  - Change 
Sustenance Post SWP of the Last Licensee Onboarded is activated. 
3.3.5. During Change Management programme implementation, the Contractor shall 
analyse issues related to system usage or adoption and propose additional change 
management measures to address these issues. 
3.3.6. The Contractor shall provide timely reviews tracking Key Performance Indicators 
(KPIs) to validate change management success and provide a post-implementation 
report one (1) month after each Licensee onboarded, including but not limited to 
the following: 
(a) Key successes, challenges, and measures of success; 
(b) Summary of findings from reviews and retrospective meetings; and 
(c) Recommendations for sustaining and reinforcing business change efforts, 
particularly focusing on the human side of change. 
3.3.7. At the Authority's request, the Contractor shall conduct briefings and updates to 
stakeholders at relevant platforms, with content ranging from project progress 
reports to sharing best practices and insights. 
3.4. Legal and Intellectual Property (IP) 
3.4.1. The Contractor shall ensure materials do not infringe third-party IP rights and shall 
indemnify the Authority from all claims arising from IP infringement.

4. CHANGE SUSTENANCE POST SWP OF THE LAST LICENSEE 
ONBOARDED  
4.1. Change Sustenance Plan Proposal 
4.1.1. The Tenderer shall propose as an option for the Authority  to exercise, a detailed 
Change Sustenance Plan which the Contractor will execute to sustain the change 
momentum, and ensure stakeholders’ continued adoption of and satisfaction with 
the Solution post SWP of the last Licensee onboarded. 
4.1.2. The Plan shall minimally include: 
(a) Stakeholder Management Plan; 
(b) Strategy and approach to sustain adoption; 
(c) Engagement and Communications Plan (including engagements during 
Solution enhancements); 
(d) Governance and Team structure; 
(e) Support framework to sustain any proposed interventions to shape desired 
behaviour; 
(f) Capability Building;  
(g) Tools and Resources; 
(h) Success measurement indicators for change interventions beyond Solution 
implementation, clearly specifying measurement objectives, measurement 
timelines, measurement indices, and appropriate follow-up actions; and  
(i) Handover plan to enable the Authority team to sustain and institutionalise the 
change within three (3) months after completing the Change Sustenance Plan.  
4.2. Plan Execution 
4.2.1. The Contractor shall refine the proposed Change Sustenance Plan during the pre-
deployment phase, taking into account feedback and measurements obtained after 
Solution training.  
4.2.2. The Contractor shall obtain the Authority’s approval for changes made to the 
Change Sustenance Plan before executing the Plan post SWP of the last Licensee 
onboarded.  
4.2.3. The Tenderer shall itemise the cost for this scope of work as an option for the 
Authority to exercise as set out in Part 3, Annex I.

5. TRAINING 
5.1. Introduction 
5.1.1. The Contractor shall provide training to meet the following five (5) main 
objectives: 
(a) To enable all Authority users and nominated personnel to manage, operate, 
configure and use the Solution effectively and proficiently; 
(b) To enable all Authority -nominated technical personnel to manage, operate, 
configure and maintain the Solution; 
(c) To enable all Authority -nominated technical personnel to have an overview 
of the system architecture and to be able to diagnose and resolve problems in 
using the Solution; 
(d) To enable a smooth transfer of knowledge from the Contractor to the 
Authority-nominated technical personnel; 
(e) Any other training and education necessary to meet the training objectives 
5.1.2. The Contractor shall provide at least the following types of training: 
(a) Product Overview and Business Process Training 
(b) Configuration for System modules Training 
(c) Business Rule Training 
(d) Application Security Administration Overview Training 
(e) System Maintenance Overview Training 
(f) Interface and Integration Training 
(g) Reports and Dashboards Overview Training 
(h) Senior Management (Pre Go-Live) Briefings 
(i) Technical Training 
(j) Training on Software and Tools 
5.1.3. The Contractor shall propose and provide suitable and adequate customised training 
for personnel nominated by the Authority to enable them to carry out smooth 
operation and management of the Solution.

5.1.4. The Authority reserves the right to accept part or all of the training courses 
proposed. 
5.1.5. The Authority may require the Contractor to make the necessary amendments to 
the training courses and training materials wherever necessary as determined by the 
Authority, and to replace any training instructor that the Authority finds 
unsatisfactory. The Contractor shall comply with any such requirement, at no 
additional costs to the Authority, within the timeframe stipulated by the Authority 
in a written notice to the Contractor. 
5.1.6. The training sessions shall be conducted at the Contractor’s site, Authority’s 
premises, Licensees’ premises, virtually, or at premises that are convenient to and 
agreed to by the Authority. 
5.1.7. If the Authority requires the training to be held in its premises, the Contractor shall 
be responsible for setting up the entire training environment. If needed, the 
Contractor shall provide all necessary training equipment that is not currently 
available in the Authority’s premises for these training purposes. 
5.1.8. The Contractor shall ensure the readiness of the training environment and inform 
the Authority of any technical deficit at least three (3) working days in advance of 
a training session. 
5.1.9. The Contractor shall ensure that all training instructor(s) are suitably qualified and 
competent, are well-spoken in English and are well-versed in the System.  
5.1.10. The medium of instruction and training materials shall be in English. 
5.1.11. For onsite trainings, the Contractor shall conduct all training sessions in Singapore. 
The Contractor shall ensure that the training materials should be readily 
comprehensible even by someone who has not attended the course(s). 
5.1.12. The Tenderer shall include in its Tender Offer the type of training, target audience, 
duration of each session, proposed number of sessions, number of trainees per 
session, schedule of the sessions, training charges and rates in the format as set out 
in Part 3, Annex I – Price Schedule and Annex VII. For avoidance of doubt, the 
Tenderer shall note that the exact number of participants for the training sessions 
will be determined by the Authority during the Contract period. 
5.1.13. The Contractor shall ensure the maximum number of participants per training 
session should not be more than thirty (30) to ensure training is delivered 
effectively, unless otherwise agreed to by the Authority. 
5.1.14. The Contractor shall provide, at no additional costs to the Authority, all necessary 
equipment, training materials and logistics coordination (e.g. inform users of 
training schedule, registration of participants, maintain records of attendance, 
collate training feedback forms, etc.) of all the training sessions.

5.1.15. The Contractor shall: 
(a) Work with the Authority to adjust details of the training sessions, including 
details such as the schedule, scope, number of sessions, number of trainees 
per sessions; 
(b) Administer the registration and attendance of trainees at all training sessions. 
5.2. Training Materials 
5.2.1. The Contractor shall provide all the training materials (inclusive of instructor's 
guide, slides and trainee's booklet) in soft copy and obtain the Authority’s Approval 
at least five (5) working days before the commencement of the training. 
5.2.2. The Authority shall own all the training materials (soft copy) developed under this 
Contract. The Authority shall have the right to use these training materials to 
conduct in-house courses for its personnel. 
5.2.3. The Contractor shall provide each trainee with a complete set of training documents 
and materials in soft copy for his retention. 
5.2.4. The Contractor shall update the training materials when the need arises , including 
to account for any new changes made to the Solution (including newly implemented 
modules, new releases / patches). 
5.3. Feedback and Evaluation 
5.3.1. The Contractor shall collect feedback from all participants of each training session 
at the end of such session, by distributing evaluation forms to all such participants. 
The Contractor shall ensure that all participants complete and submit evaluation 
forms in a training session. The Contractor shall collate the results of the evaluation 
forms and submit the completed evaluation forms to the Authority for its review 
within seven (7) days after the end of each training session. 
5.3.2. If more than fifty percent (50%) of the feedback gathered on any training session 
conducted by the Contractor is rated three or below (based on a six -point rating 
scale), the Contractor shall, if so required by the Authority, re-conduct such training 
sessions at no additional costs to the Authority. 
5.4. Application 
5.4.1. The Contractor shall provide training on the application on various roles such as 
users, administrator, operators, etc.

6. ORIENTATION PROGRAMME 
6.1. General Requirements for Orientation Programme 
6.1.1. Prior to the commencement of application software development activities, the 
Contractor shall provide and deliver, at no additional cost to the Authority, an 
orientation programme for the Authority’s personnel who are identified to be in the 
project team, in order to familiarise and prepare these personnel on project and 
product management activities as provided by the Contractor. 
6.1.2. The Contractor shall provide the necessary manpower and logistics to support the 
end-to-end delivery of the proposed orientation programme, which may include the 
following: 
(a) Venue where the orientation programme would be conducted (if the proposed 
venue is not at the Authority’s premises); 
(b) Printing of presentation or other training/ reference materials; and 
(c) Coaches to facilitate group discussion activities and gamification. 
6.1.3. The Tenderer shall provide the following information about the proposed 
orientation programme into the Tender Offer: 
(a) Total duration of the orientation programme; 
(b) Proposed number of personnel from the Authority’s organisation who may 
attend the orientation programme;  
(c) Proposed venue where the orientation programme would be conducted; and 
(d) Broad-level overview of the orientation programme schedule, including the 
knowledge areas that would be covered, modes of information delivery, types 
of activities, and approximate durations of the various portions of the 
orientation. 
6.1.4. The Tenderer shall propose whether there is a need to have differentiated 
orientation programmes for the Authority’s working team and the Authority’s 
management team. If so, the Tenderer shall articulate the portions that are different 
as separate tracks, in the proposed orientation programme schedule.  
6.1.5. The orientation programme shall equip the Authority’s personnel with the relevant 
capabilities for effective and efficient involvement in the development phase of the 
project. The programme should include the following:  
(a) Aligning the project , product management, Agile methodology and event 
milestones (e.g. team organisation structure, prioritised product backlog, 
release plan, sprint backlog, development processes).

(b) Sharing roles and responsibilities of key roles in the project (e.g. Product 
Owner, Business Users, Product Manager, Agile Coach, and the

(c) Defining Sprint Duration, Definition of Done, Calendar of Ceremonies (e.g.
