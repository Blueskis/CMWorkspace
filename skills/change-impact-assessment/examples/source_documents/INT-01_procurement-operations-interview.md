# Interview Notes — Procurement Operations
**Ref:** INT-01
**Date:** 2026-06-04
**Participants:** Head of Procurement Operations; 2 × Senior Buyer; 1 × Buying Team Lead (EMEA)
**Interviewer:** CM Lead

---

## How buying works today

Requisitions arrive by email to the shared procurement mailbox — roughly 900/month across EMEA.
No standard form. "About a third of them we have to go back to the requester because there's
no cost centre or no spec." Buying team manually keys the requisition into ECC (ME51N) after
chasing the missing data.

Approval today: buyers check the DOA spreadsheet on SharePoint (maintained by Finance,
"last properly updated maybe 18 months ago") to work out who signs off, then email the approver.
Approvals come back as email replies which buyers save into the shared drive as evidence.
Team Lead: "Audit hates it. We know."

Catalogues: there are none. Everything is free-text. Buyers keep personal spreadsheets of
preferred suppliers and last-agreed prices. Senior Buyer: "My price list is in my head and in
a spreadsheet on my desktop. If I'm off, the team guesses."

## Sourcing

RFQs are run over email. Buyer builds a comparison spreadsheet manually, emails suppliers
individually, collates responses. Typical event: 3–5 suppliers, 2 weeks.
"Nobody except me sees the bids until I've made the recommendation." Head of Procurement noted
this is a known audit finding — no independent visibility of bid data.

Second-round negotiation happens on the phone. Nothing is recorded except the final number.

## Contracts

Contracts live in a shared drive folder structure by supplier name. No expiry alerting.
"We find out a contract lapsed when the supplier calls about a PO we can't raise."
Estimated 400 active contracts; nobody has a definitive count.

## Team and headcount

- 14 buyers across EMEA (UK 6, DE 4, PL 4)
- 3 Buying Team Leads
- 1 Head of Procurement Operations
- Category Managers sit separately — 8 across 4 categories

## What they said about the change

Head of Procurement is supportive: "We've asked for a system for four years."

Senior Buyers are noticeably more guarded. Direct quote: *"So the system decides the price
now? What am I for?"* — this came up twice, unprompted, from different people. There is a
clear concern that catalogue-driven buying removes the buyer's role in transactional purchasing.

Team Lead (EMEA), on the previous rollout: *"We did this with the last ERP. Two days of
training six weeks before go-live, then nothing. Half the team still doesn't use it properly."*

Poland team raised that all training in the previous programme was in English only and
delivered UK-time. Flagged as a live concern for this rollout.
