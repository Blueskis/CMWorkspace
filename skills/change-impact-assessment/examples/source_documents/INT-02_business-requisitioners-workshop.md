# Workshop Notes — Business Requisitioners & Budget Approvers
**Ref:** INT-02
**Date:** 2026-06-11
**Participants:** 22 attendees — Engineering (6), Marketing (4), Facilities (3), IT (4), R&D (5).
Mix of requisitioners and cost-centre owners.
**Facilitator:** CM Lead

---

## Today's experience

Requisitioners describe buying as "emailing procurement and hoping". No visibility of status
once the email is sent. Engineering rep: "I chase on day 10, day 15 and day 20. That's my process."

Average quoted lead time from request to PO: 12–18 working days for non-urgent, "a phone call
and a favour" for urgent.

Requisitioner population is broad and occasional — most people in the room raise fewer than
10 requisitions a year. Facilities and IT are the exceptions (several per week).

Estimated total requisitioner population confirmed by HR extract: **~3,800 employees** have
raised at least one request in the last 12 months. Of those, ~180 raise more than one a month.

## Approvals

Cost centre owners approve by replying to a buyer's email. Several admitted they approve
without seeing much detail — "if it's from my team and the number looks right, I say yes."
No line-item visibility.

~640 employees hold approval authority per the HR/Finance extract. Approval limits vary by
grade but nobody in the room could state their own limit correctly when asked.

Marketing rep noted they frequently split purchases to stay under their limit. Said openly,
in the room, with no apparent awareness it was a control issue.

## Reaction to what they'd been told about the new system

Broadly positive on visibility and speed. "If I could see where my order is, that alone would
be worth it."

Two concerns raised repeatedly:

1. **"Another system to learn."** Several people pointed out they raise four requisitions a
   year and will have forgotten how by the next one. R&D rep: "Don't send me on a course in
   October for something I'll use in March."
2. **Catalogue restriction.** Engineering pushed back hard on the idea that they'd have to buy
   from a catalogue: "Half of what I buy is bespoke. If the system won't let me describe what
   I actually need, I'll go around it." Facilities agreed.

## Approvers specifically

When told approvals would move into the system with enforced limits and no ability to approve
outside their DOA, reaction was mild — most assumed this was already the case. The finding
worth noting is that **enforcement is a bigger change than they realise**; several are used to
approving things they technically cannot.

## Open items
