Where each group stands

Every score normalised to 0–100, higher is better. A base under 5 responses is marked thin and never shown as on track, however high it scores.

How the answers split

An average hides a divided room. Each bar is the share of responses that were negative, neutral, or positive on that dimension — two groups pulling apart look nothing like a room that is mildly lukewarm, and they need opposite responses.

What stands out

Computed from the numbers above, not judged. Each one names the cells behind it so you can check it. Read these as prompts for the conversation, not as conclusions.

What people wrote

Grouped so you can code them yourself. The word counts are a hint about where to look — they are not themes. “Training” appearing forty times means nothing; eleven people describing the same unusable exercise means a great deal.

Take it further

This page reads one snapshot on its own. To turn it into a briefed set of insights — themed comments, findings anchored to milestones with an action window, and an audit that fails when a blind spot goes undeclared — export the summary and hand it to the practitioner running the full readiness pipeline.

A quick look, not an assessment. This page computes what the responses say. It cannot tell you whether the instrument asked the right question, whether the people who did not respond think something different, or what to do by when.

Bands (70 / 55) and the thin-base floor (n = 5) are conventions, not findings. Say so when someone quotes a colour at you.
