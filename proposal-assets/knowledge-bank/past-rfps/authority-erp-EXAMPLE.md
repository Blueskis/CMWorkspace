## The terminology this authority used

Their term is the section name, per Rule 1 of the section library. This authority's
mapping, worth reusing on anything else they issue:

| We call it | They called it |
|---|---|
| CM Plan and Strategy | Change Management Strategy and Approach |
| Stakeholder Analysis and Engagement | Detailed Stakeholder Engagement Plan |
| Change Agent Network | Change Agent Identification and Training |
| Adoption / Evaluation Metrics | Success Measurement Framework |
| Knowledge Transfer | Handover plan |

They also named three deliverables the canonical ten do not carry — Transition Management
plan, Change Intervention plan, Change Sustenance Plan — each of which needed its own home
in the response.

## Requirement patterns that recurred

- **Training was specified operationally, not conceptually.** Class sizes, languages,
  venue standards, instructor qualifications, a feedback threshold above which a session
  must be re-run at no cost. Each is a discrete requirement and each was scored
  separately. Responses that compressed these into "role-based training" lost the marks.
- **"At no additional cost to the Authority"** appeared six times, attached to different
  obligations. It is a commercial constraint, not a scope item, and belongs in the
  commercials section as an explicit acknowledgement.
- **Cross-references to parts not issued with the chapter.** Role descriptions lived in
  another chapter, pricing formats in an annex. Three requirements could not be answered
  without them, and the clarification deadline was three weeks before submission.

## What the debrief said to change

The handover section described what knowledge transfer is rather than when it would
happen and who would sign it off. The tender had specified "within three months before the
end of the warranty period" and the response did not mirror that timing anywhere. Next
time: take the client's stated deadline and put it on the slide.
