> **This is a format exemplar with invented content. Delete before real use.**

## The block

> **If you need help**
>
> Contact [service desk name] on [address or number], [hours]. They can help with [the
> specific thing this change makes people do].
>
> If your [action] isn't working after [reasonable interval], [escalation route].

## Why it is shaped this way

**Name what they can help with.** A bare address makes the reader decide whether their problem
qualifies, and the ones who decide it doesn't are the ones who then miss the deadline.

**State the hours.** A comm sent at 5pm on a Friday with a support line that opens Monday
should say so, or it generates a weekend of unanswered attempts and a Monday backlog.

**Give an escalation.** The first-line route will fail for a predictable fraction of people. If
the comm doesn't say what happens then, they escalate to whoever signed the email, which is
both slower and worse.

## Alternative formats

Where the comm goes to a whole employee base, pair this with the accessibility block. Offering
an alternative format only on request still requires the person to know they can ask.

## What to check before shipping it

The address and hours are current. A support route that changed in the last reorganisation and
was copied forward from a previous comm is a common and entirely avoidable failure — the
practitioner should confirm it against a live source, not against the last email that used it.
