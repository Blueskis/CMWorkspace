> **This is a format exemplar with invented content. Delete before real use.**

## When this structure works

A system goes live on a known date, and every affected person has one thing to do before it
does. The comm has to survive being read in eight seconds on a phone, so the action goes above
everything except the reason the reader should care.

## The sequence that worked

1. **Subject** — the action and the deadline, not the programme name. "Enrol in the new portal
   by 14 September" outperformed "Project Horizon: Phase 2 update" substantially on open rate.
2. **Preheader** — who it affects. Lets people who aren't affected stop reading, which is a
   feature.
3. **One line on why now.** Not the business case; the reason *this email exists today*.
4. **The action, with the effort stated.** "Takes about ten minutes." Naming the effort raised
   completion noticeably — people defer an unbounded task and forget it.
5. **What happens if you don't.** Factual, not threatening. People need the consequence to
   prioritise, and inventing urgency where there is none is how a programme loses credibility
   for its next send.
6. **What isn't changing.** Two lines. Absorbs most of the questions that would otherwise
   arrive at the service desk.
7. **Where to get help**, with hours.
8. **A named human**, with their role.

## What we would change

The timeline table was below the help section and most readers never reached it. Move dates
directly under the action.

Avoid "further details will follow" without a date attached — it generated a second wave of
queries asking when the details were coming, which is a self-inflicted wound.
