## Diagnostic before prescription

Engagements open with a structured diagnostic rather than a standard playbook, so the
approach is sized to the change actually in front of us. A system rollout with settled
processes needs a different shape from a reorganisation that asks people to give something
up, and treating both the same is the most common reason change budgets get spent without
changing anything.

## Networks over hierarchies

Engagement runs through volunteer change-agent networks recruited from the affected
population, not through cascaded briefings down the org chart. Cascades reliably lose
fidelity at every layer; a network of people who volunteered keeps it.

## Measurement built in from the start

Adoption measures are defined during Assess and baselined before go-live, so the programme
can tell adoption from compliance. Retrofitting measurement after go-live produces numbers
nobody trusts.

## Handover as a deliverable

Every engagement has a named business owner for each change artifact from the outset, and
handover is a phase-gated deliverable rather than a closing activity. Change that depends
on the consultants staying is not change.
