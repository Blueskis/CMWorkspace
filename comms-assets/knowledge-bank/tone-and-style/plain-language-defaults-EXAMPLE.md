## Defaults, unless the brief states a house style that overrides them

**Active voice, named actor.** "The finance team will migrate your data" not "your data
will be migrated." An audience trusts a communication more when it says who is doing the
thing, not just that the thing will happen to them.

**Second person for the audience, first person plural for the sender's own actions.**
"You'll need to log in with your existing credentials. We're running the migration
overnight on 14 September." Mixing this up reads as evasive.

**No unexplained acronyms on first use**, even ones the internal team treats as obvious.
Spell out the system name once per channel, then abbreviate. This is also what the
`glossary/` folder exists to standardise across channels in one run.

**Sentences under 25 words where the content allows it.** Not a hard rule — a genuinely
complex dependency sometimes needs a longer sentence — but the default should be short,
especially in email and banner copy where a reader skims rather than reads.

**Confirmed vs. unconfirmed gets a consistent phrase, not a synonym per channel.** Use
"confirmed" and "provisional, subject to change" (or an equivalent pair a house style
supplies) the same way in every channel of a run — inconsistent phrasing here is exactly
what Stage 5's cross-channel consistency check flags.

## Why this entry is `internal-only`

It states house defaults, not client-facing content — nothing here should be quoted
directly into a client-facing pack; it's read, not retrieved into a block's `sources`
the way a narrative or channel-example entry would be.
